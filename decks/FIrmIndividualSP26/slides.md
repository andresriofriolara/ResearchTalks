## Budget balance: allocations exhaust firm payoff

<div class="b3-wrap">
<div class="b3-band fragment" data-fragment-index="0">
    <strong>Budget balance: the allocations exhaust firm payoff.</strong>
    <div class="b3-equation">
      \[
        \sum_{g\in G}\Lambda_g(Y,\upsilon)=Y
      \]
    </div>
    <div class="b3-sub">
      Sum the allocated payoffs across all coalitions in G.
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="b3-source">
    Sharing is Pareto efficient, budget balanced,
    and supported by decision-independent welfare weights.
    Source: Section 3.1.
  </div>
</div>

Note:
The construction is recursive. Individuals form coalition syndicates.
Coalitions whose evaluations admit a surrogate utility and probability
assessment enter the firm tier as its constituent syndicates.

Y is total firm payoff; υ is the aggregate state.
Λg allocates payoff to coalition g.
σg is the derivative of that allocation with respect to total payoff.

Distinguish the marginal share σg from the allocated payoff Λg.
In the CARA example, the marginal share equals the coalition's
share of total risk tolerance.

## The CARA bank: risk tolerance and belief weights

<div class="b3-wrap">
<div class="b3-band fragment" data-fragment-index="0">
    <strong>In the CARA bank:</strong>
    <div class="b3-grid" style="margin-top:10px;">
      <div>
        <div class="b3-equation">
          \[
            \kappa_F=\sum_g\kappa_g
          \]
        </div>
        <div class="b3-equation-caption">
          κ (kappa) denotes constant risk tolerance.
          The firm’s κ<sub>F</sub> is the sum of
          its coalitions’ κ<sub>g</sub>.
        </div>
      </div>
      <div>
        <div class="b3-equation">
          \[
            \sigma_g=\frac{\kappa_g}{\kappa_F}
          \]
        </div>
        <div class="b3-equation-caption">
          σ<sub>g</sub> (sigma), the coalition’s marginal payoff
          share and belief weight, equals its share
          of total risk tolerance.
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="b3-source">
    CARA: constant absolute risk aversion.
    With agreement, the firm uses the common probability assessment.
    Source: Section 3.2, Definitions 1–2.
  </div>
</div>

Note:
The sum of local risk tolerances and the existence of a stable
firm-level utility are distinct claims.

Under agreement, efficient sharing makes allocations depend on total
payoff alone. Under heterogeneous beliefs, the paper identifies the
linearity condition supporting firm-level factorization.

The stock-weighted geometric expression shown here applies under
linear and determinate sharing.

In the CARA calibration, tolerances and coalition stocks are constant.
The bank has κF = 9 and weights 1/3 and 2/3.

## Unanimity: a shared ranking of firm acts

<div class="b3-wrap">
<div class="b3-purple-band b3-band fragment" data-fragment-index="0">
    Different beliefs and information can remain.<br>
    <strong>The induced ranking of firm acts is shared.</strong>
  </div>
  <div class="b3-source">

    before adding private blame costs.

Note:
Linearity concerns independence from aggregate payoff.
Determinacy concerns independence from the aggregate state.

Keep factorization and unanimity distinct:
under heterogeneous beliefs, linearity supplies the paper's
factorization condition, while linear and determinate sharing jointly
supply the stronger unanimity result.

Unanimity means a common induced ranking over firm acts.
It does not require identical original beliefs.

Under the baseline construction, every firm optimum is preferred
by all coalitions. Private costs outside shared output will create
a separate issue later in this block.

## Decisional unity: implementing the firm’s criterion

<div class="b3-wrap">
<div class="b3-band fragment" data-fragment-index="0">
    <strong>Decisional unity:</strong>
    the organization implements an optimal admissible rule<br>
    according to its own firm-level criterion.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-source">
    “∈ arg max” means the selected rule belongs to the set of
    maximizing rules; the optimum need not be unique.
    Source: Section 3.3.
  </div>
</div>

Note:
The architecture comprises observation, communication, delegation,
and reporting.

A team decision rule maps the histories generated by that architecture
into firm actions. Each position's component must respect the
information available to it.

The maximization is over admissible rules under the actual architecture.
Decisional unity does not require attaining the value available under
a different, more informative architecture.

Multiple optimal rules may exist; implementing any optimum satisfies
the condition.

## When is the firm an individual?

<div class="b3-wrap">
  <div class="b3-definition">
    <strong>Definition 4 · Individual representability</strong><br><br>
    One utility and one probability assessment rationalize the
    implemented rule across every decision problem
    in the model’s admissible domain.
  </div>
  <div class="fragment" data-fragment-index="0">
    <div class="b3-band">
      <strong>Evaluative unity</strong><br>
      A stable firm-level utility and probability assessment exist.
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="fragment" data-fragment-index="1">
    <div class="b3-and">AND</div>
    <div class="b3-band b3-purple-band" style="margin-top:0;">
      <strong>Decisional unity</strong><br>
      The implemented rule maximizes that criterion<br>
      within the prevailing architecture.
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="b3-takeaway fragment" data-fragment-index="2">
    Firm individuality ⇔ both conditions hold.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-source">
    The equivalence is stated within the paper’s model and admissible
    decision domain. Source: Sections 3.3–3.4 and Definition 4.
  </div>
</div>

Note:
The same pair must rationalize behavior across the admissible domain.
Fitting a separate evaluator to each isolated choice is insufficient.

The utility belongs to the class used by the paper's construction:
increasing and concave, with the stated strictness and regularity
conditions.

The main text separates the two clauses through its decision domain.
Evaluative unity is identified on binary problems with constant
admissible rules and no blame cost. Decisional unity concerns the
remaining problems involving distributed information.

This slide states the main-text result. It does not reproduce
the supplementary proof or additional domain formalization.

## Failure A: the evaluator stops being stable

<div class="b3-wrap">
  <div class="b3-context">
    Return to the bank.
    Protect loan production below a <strong>−$3.6 million payoff floor</strong>.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-grid">
    <div class="b3-card">
      <div class="b3-label">FLOOR SLACK</div>
      <div class="b3-main">Original sharing operates</div>
      <div class="b3-equation">
        \[
          R_F(G)=R_F(B)=9
        \]
      </div>
      <div class="b3-sub">
        At the $6 million credit, loan production’s worst-state
        share is −$2.89 million.
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="b3-card b3-orange-card fragment" data-fragment-index="0">
      <div class="b3-label">FLOOR BINDS IN THE BAD STATE</div>
      <div class="b3-main">Marginal downside shifts</div>
      <div class="b3-equation">
        \[
          R_F(G)=9,\qquad R_F(B)=6
        \]
      </div>
      <div class="b3-sub">
        Risk management absorbs the additional downside.
        Firm risk tolerance becomes state-dependent.
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="b3-band b3-orange-band fragment" data-fragment-index="1">
    The revealed belief changes with the stake.<br>
    <strong>0.675 → 0.5896</strong> at a $45 million exposure.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-takeaway fragment" data-fragment-index="2">
    No single stable utility–belief pair<br>
    rationalizes choices across these acts.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-source">
    The floor first binds on the credit ray at E = 13.052 million.
    Source: Section 4.4. The displayed drift follows the credit ray.
  </div>

Note:
The floor is initially slack, so merely introducing it does not
immediately destroy the original representation.

Once it binds in the bad state, marginal risk sharing changes across
states. The evaluation measure no longer factors into the stable
surrogate utility and probability assessment of the baseline.

The diagnostic is a violation of prize-independence with information
held fixed, not ordinary belief updating after new evidence.

Do not confuse the credit ray with Figure 6's certainty ray:
their paths and limiting values differ.

At the worked credit's odds, the bank's approval frontier lies below
the floor-binding threshold. Passive approve/decline observations can
therefore conceal the failure.

## Failure B: agreement survives, implementation fails

<div class="b3-wrap">
  <div class="b3-context">
    Return to pooled information.
    Add a private, act-dependent blame cost outside shared payoff Y.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-definition">
    Allocate blame in proportion to marginal payoff shares:
    <strong>b<sub>g</sub> = σ<sub>g</sub></strong>.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-equation">
    \[
      W_g(\delta)=\sigma_g
      \left\{
        \mathbb E_{Q_F}
        \!\left[V_F(H(\upsilon,\delta(h)))\mid\mathcal A\right]
        -\theta B(\delta)
      \right\}
    \]
  </div>
  <div class="b3-sub" style="text-align:center;">
    θ is blame-cost intensity; B(δ) is the rule’s expected blame exposure.
  </div>
  <div class="b3-grid" style="margin-top:22px;">
    <div class="b3-card fragment" data-fragment-index="0">
      <div class="b3-label">BELOW THE THRESHOLD</div>
      <div class="b3-main">Implement the team optimum</div>
      <div class="b3-sub">
        Firm value: <strong>0.3672</strong>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="b3-card b3-orange-card fragment" data-fragment-index="1">

  </div>
  <div class="b3-band b3-orange-band fragment" data-fragment-index="2">
    The coalitions still agree with one another.<br>
    <strong>Their implemented rule falls short of the firm’s optimum.</strong>
  </div>
  <div class="b3-source">
    Evaluative unity remains intact; decisional unity fails.
    Source: Sections 3.2–3.3 and the numerical discussion in Section 4.5.

Note:
The cost is borne privately and is outside the output Y that the
syndicate shares. It changes coalition incentives while leaving the
original firm-level evaluator intact.

When bg = σg, every coalition's criterion is a positive multiple
of the same expression. Their rankings remain identical to one
another, although that common ranking can depart from the original
firm criterion.

On the split profile, incremental firm value is 0.0483 and incremental
blame exposure is 0.2457. Their ratio gives θ* = 0.1966.

Below the threshold, the tremble-robust rule is the team optimum.
Above it, both coalitions prefer to veto the split profile.
Pooled information removes architecture loss from this comparison.

The displayed values follow the main text's blame-cost calibration.
They are distinct from the rounded pooled value in Table 5.
The inconsistent embedded plot in Figure 8 is not reproduced here.
